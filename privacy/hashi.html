Hashi

Privacy policy

Last updated 16 November 2026. Applies to the Android game Hashi, package com.anubisproductions.hashi, published by Anubis Productions, Cairo, Egypt.

In short

Hashi is free to play and paid for by advertising. It saves your progress to our server so it survives a new phone and so the weekly tournament can be ranked. It never asks for your name or your email address, and there is no sign-up.

What we store about you

When the game first runs it signs in anonymously with Google Firebase, which issues a random identifier for that installation. It is not your Google account and not your device's advertising identifier. Everything we keep is filed against that identifier:

  • your progress: levels solved, stars, best times, coins, streak, achievements;
  • the puzzle you are in the middle of;
  • your weekly tournament score, with a display name and an emblem. The game makes the name from that same identifier, such as “Swift Ibis”, and shows it to other players on the weekly standings board. You do not choose it and you cannot change it: there is no field anywhere in the game that takes a name, so nothing you write can reach another player.

Your settings and a copy of the same progress are also held on the phone itself, and uninstalling removes that copy.

Who else sees data

  • Google Firebase stores the records above on our behalf, in Google's European region, and sees the technical details any internet request carries, such as your IP address. See the Firebase privacy documentation.
  • Google AdMob supplies the advertising. AdMob may use your device's advertising identifier to choose and measure ads, as described in how Google uses data from apps that use its advertising services. Where the law requires it, the game asks for your consent before requesting a personalised ad, and you can change that choice later from the game's settings.

Nobody else. We do not sell data, and we do not share it for anyone else's advertising.

What the game does not collect

  • No real name, email address or account of any kind. The display name above is made from the identifier rather than typed by you, and the game never asks who you are.
  • No contacts, photos, files, location, microphone or camera. The only Android permission the game asks for itself is internet access. Google's advertising library adds a few of its own when the app is built: network state, wake lock, a background-work service and the advertising identifier. They are listed in full on the app's Play listing under "App permissions".
  • No analytics or crash reporting beyond what Firebase and AdMob need to run.

This website

Everything above is about the Android game. This site, anubisproductions.github.io, is measured separately with Google Analytics so we can see how many people reach a page and which link brought them there.

Until you answer the strip at the foot of the page it runs cookieless. It records that a page was viewed, along with the country, the browser and the page that linked here, and writes nothing to your device. Accepting allows one cookie, which is what lets a return visit be told apart from a new one. Declining leaves it cookieless for good. Your answer is kept in your own browser's storage, which is why you are asked once; clearing this site's data asks again.

The copy of the browser build published on itch.io loads the same file from this site and is measured the same way, under the same consent, so that a game played there is not invisible to us. Clicking any link from these pages to Google Play is recorded as a click, with the page it was on. The click is counted; who clicked is not.

Nothing here is used for advertising. There are no ads on this site, and the advertising categories of consent stay denied whichever button you press. The links from these pages to Google Play carry a campaign tag that tells us which page sent a visit, and Play reports that back to us as a count, never as a person.

Children

Hashi is not directed at children under 13.

Deleting your data

Write to anubisproductionsmarketing@gmail.com and ask. Since nothing we hold carries your name, quote the display name the tournament shows you so we can find the record. Uninstalling the game removes the copy on your phone and stops anything further being stored.

Changes

If this policy changes the date at the top changes with it, and the earlier wording stays in the history of the public repository that serves this page.
